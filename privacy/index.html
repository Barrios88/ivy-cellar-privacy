The Ivy Cellar — Privacy Policy

Last updated 24 November 2026

In short: The Ivy Cellar has no servers of its own and collects nothing about you. Your cellar lives on your devices and in your own iCloud account. The app has no ads, no analytics and no tracking.

Your cellar

Everything you put in the app — wines, bottles, places, notes, pictures, settings — is stored on your device. If you're signed in to iCloud, it is also kept in your private iCloud database so your devices stay in step. Apple holds that data under Apple's privacy policy; the developer can't see it.

Backups you make are files you save where you choose. AI keys you enter are kept in your device's Keychain and never leave it except to reach the AI provider you chose.

Sharing with friends

Nothing is shared unless you invite someone. When you do, that person — and only that person — can see a read-only copy of what you hold: wines and bottle counts by size, drinking windows, critics' scores and your research. Prices, values, where bottles are kept, barcodes, your own scores and tasting notes are never shared. The copy travels through iCloud sharing. You can stop sharing with anyone at any time; stopping can't take back what they've already seen.

Optional AI features

Reading labels and receipts, researching wines and producers, and the sommelier are optional. They only run when you set them up with your own account or key. When you use them, the label photo, receipt text or wine details needed for that request are sent from your device directly to the provider you chose — Anthropic (Claude) or OpenAI — under that provider's terms and privacy policy. They are not sent to the developer.

Maps and pictures

To place wineries on the map, a winery's name and address are looked up with Apple's map search. The map itself is drawn from tiles fetched from OpenFreeMap and a public terrain-tile service; those requests carry the map area being viewed, not anything about you or your cellar. When you ask the app to find a picture of a label, it opens the web pages your research cited to look for one.

Children

The Ivy Cellar is about wine and is meant for adults of legal drinking age.
